 2. The scoring is not what you planned for:
  - Metric: scoring uses MAE only, so you can drop RMSE. Each metric gets a
    skill score, S = 1 − MAE / MAE(30-yr avg), and the final Score is 100 × the
    mean of the four skills. Write this as a function and use it on every fold.
  - Official baselines:
    - The 30-year average uses the fixed years 1989–2018, not "the last 30
      training years".
    - The trend line is fit through the last 30 years, not from 1970 onward.
    - There's a third baseline: repeat each city's last valid value.
  - Free reproducibility check: the organizers ran a practice fold (train up to
    2010, predict 2011–2018). It gave CDD MAE of 213 for the 30-year average and
    177 for the trend line. Add that fold and confirm you get the same numbers.
  - More folds: two 7-year folds is thin evidence. Add 1997 and 2010 as extra
    cutoffs.

  3. Items you can now close or drop:
  - Format check (Day 4): the format is city_id,year,metric,predicted_value.
    city_id is the station ID, and all 5,880 rows are required.
  - "Unclear submission requirements" feedback: that's resolved now. Replace it

  - Uncertainty intervals (Day 4): not required. Treat them as optional extra

  - SimpleImputer: you probably don't need an imputer at all.

  4. Nothing in the plan aims at Reuse (25%) or Interoperability (20%). That's
  45% of the rubric. Some cheap ideas:
  - Write the forecaster as a small reusable function or module, e.g.
    forecast(city_id, metric, cutoff), instead of code that only lives in the
    notebook.
  - Combine with an external source from a different agency, such as Census
    population or NLCD imperviousness/tree canopy. Population-weighted CDD
    exposure would feed straight into the Policy Idea.
  - Pin the dataset version with load_dataset(..., revision="<commit>"), set

  5. Every row needs a prediction. A few cities don't have enough history and
  won't be scored, but you still have to fill them. Plan a fallback, such as a
  regional or nearest-neighbor mean.

  6. Timeline: set up the HF dataset from the template on Day 2, not Day 6, and
  submit early (up to 10 times a day). The live leaderboard only scores
  2019–2021, and the winners are chosen on 2022–2025, so don't tune to the live
  score.

  Modeling idea for Day 3: fitting a separate 30-year trend per city is noisy.
  Shrinking each city's slope toward a regional or national pooled slope, then
  adding it to a recent 10–15-year mean, usually beats both baselines on
  temperature and CDD. It's also easy to explain under Technical Creativity.
